# The pocket

The part that cannot be measured and the reason varied sentence length still reads dead.

## The problem with the standard advice

"Vary your sentence length" is correct and insufficient. Follow it exactly and you get prose that alternates predictably, which is its own kind of metronome. Short, long, short, long. The reader clocks the pattern within three paragraphs and stops being surprised by anything.

A pattern the reader can predict is a pattern the reader stops hearing. Variation is not the goal. Variation the reader cannot anticipate is the goal.

## What the pocket is

In music, the pocket is where the beat is and where a performer sits relative to it. Dead on the beat is machine-accurate and lifeless. Slightly ahead is urgent, pushing, anxious. Slightly behind is relaxed, confident, heavy. The same notes played in three different relationships to the same beat produce three different feelings.

Prose has the same thing. The reader builds an expectation of when the sentence will resolve, usually within a clause or two, and the writer either meets it, beats it, or makes them wait.

**On time** is the sentence that ends exactly where the reader thought it would. Correct. Forgettable. Most writing, all of the time.

**Early** is the sentence that stops before the reader expected. It feels like an interruption, a decision, a door closing. Used for a verdict.

```
On time:  "The campaign did not perform as well as we had hoped."
Early:    "The campaign died."
```

**Late** is the sentence that keeps going past the point it could have stopped, and the extra piece is the one that matters.

```
On time:  "We lost the account because the work was late."
Late:     "We lost the account because the work was late, which everyone
           knew in March."
```

The late version holds its real content in the tail. The reader thought they had the sentence, and did not.

## The measurable shadow: how a sentence lands

Full prosody is not computable without a pronunciation dictionary, but one component is, and it carries most of the weight: the final syllable.

**Hard landing.** The sentence ends on a stressed, usually one-syllable, content word. The sound stops. `It worked.` `Nobody came.` `She quit.`

**Soft landing.** The sentence ends on an unstressed syllable or a function word. The sound fades out. `It was working.` `Nobody was coming.` `She was thinking about it.`

**Mid.** A multisyllable content word that neither stops dead nor trails. `The campaign collapsed.`

The script classifies every sentence as one of the three. A passage where almost nothing lands hard never stops moving, which the reader experiences as a lack of conviction. There is no point in it where the writer appears to have decided something.

The fix is almost always in the verb. Soft landings come from progressive and passive constructions. `It was unsuccessful` lands soft because the last stressed syllable is buried three syllables from the end. `It failed` lands hard. Same claim, different conviction, and the difference is phonetic rather than semantic.

The opposite failure exists. Every sentence landing hard is relentless, and relentless reads as either a motivational poster or someone shouting. Punches need setups.

## Three moves that create pocket

**Delay the resolution.** Hold the thing the sentence is about until the end. The reader waits, and the wait is the effect. Works once or twice in a passage, becomes a tic after three.

**Resolve too early, then add.** End the sentence where it could have ended. Then add a short piece after the period that would have fit inside it. The period is doing the work. `We shipped it. Eventually.`

**Break the pattern you just built.** Give the reader two sentences with the same shape so they predict the third, then do something else. This is the only one of the three that depends on what came before, which is why it cannot be checked sentence by sentence and has to be heard across a paragraph.

## How to check it

Read the passage out loud at the speed you would actually say it, not at skim speed. Mark every place your voice did something you did not plan. Those are the live spots. A passage with no live spots is on the beat the whole way through, and that is the diagnosis even when every score is good.

This is the one thing in the skill that the script cannot do.
